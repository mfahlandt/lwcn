## 👋 Welcome

This week in Cloud Native saw a broad focus on security, with numerous projects releasing updates to address a recently disclosed certificate authority compromise. The ecosystem also continued to explore the evolving relationship between Kubernetes and AI workloads, including new open-source tools for managing AI inference and agents. Discussions also covered broader open-source supply chain security and the impact of AI on vulnerability discovery.

## 📰 This Week in Cloud Native

This week saw a significant number of open-source projects issue security updates, primarily in response to the compromise of the eMudhra intermediate Certificate Authority (CA). Projects like cert-manager, Cilium, Crossplane, Dapr, Envoy, Prometheus, SPIRE, Atlantis, Dex, Kagent, K8up, and Trivy released patch versions to update their Go toolchains (often to Go 1.26.10 or 1.27.3) and distrust the compromised eMudhra CA certificates. This widespread effort aims to mitigate risks associated with potentially fraudulent certificates in TLS communication across the cloud native stack. Beyond this specific incident, the OpenSSF released a guide on leveraging AI for finding and fixing software vulnerabilities, acknowledging that AI is accelerating the discovery of flaws. The OpenSSF also introduced the Gemara project to clarify security attestations and streamline compliance with regulations like the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA), emphasizing the importance of supply chain security and upstream contributions for reducing technical debt.

The integration of AI workloads within Kubernetes continued to be a central theme. CNCF blogs discussed how Kubernetes autoscaling is being reshaped by the demands of AI workloads and strategies for multi-tenant GPU Kubernetes deployments using open-source projects like Kamaji and HAMi. New open-source frameworks and tools emerged to support AI applications on Kubernetes, including LLM-D 1.0 for production AI inference, Kaito for deploying model weights and managing inference, and Agent Substrate for actor CPU and memory limits. Additionally, the open-source community saw the release of Omnara as an alternative for running durable AI agents, Pando as a platform for deploying AI-built apps, and Ternlux for building local-first AI pipelines. OpenAI's "Open Responses" specification for a common LLM API was donated to the Agentic AI Foundation, signaling a move towards open standards in the AI agent ecosystem. TorchTPU was also open-sourced, enabling native PyTorch execution on TPUs. The OpenTelemetry Collector was highlighted for its role in filtering and reducing telemetry from AI agents, providing a technical pathway for managing the increased data volume generated by agentic workflows.

The broader open-source ecosystem also saw new tooling and reflections on its evolution. A new open-source Kubernetes desktop client, Jet Pilot, was showcased, alongside Stackdome, a self-hostable Railway alternative on Kubernetes. KiviDB, a Redis-compatible memory database and vector cache, was released. Other tools included Craig, a TUI for managing parallel AI coding agents, and Corgi, an agent/terminal multiplexer. The CNCF reflected on 10 years of Cloud Native, noting the evolution of Kubernetes and the concept of "invisible infrastructure."
